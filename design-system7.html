Principles

Agreed with the client and Bentley's guidelines, in force on every page. When something cannot follow them, ask before inventing.

  1. Strictly Bentley style. Every element comes from Bentley's Retailer Website Guidelines, bentleymotors.com's own patterns or the approved pages here. No rule, mark, ornament or device of our own.
  2. A static page. The clientele is older, on desktop and phone: no scroll effects, no reveals, no sticking chapters, no autoplaying slider. Slides turn when the visitor turns them. The one exception is a model page's hero loop (see Model pages).
  3. Photographs are the design. Bentley Motors' own (the retailer is authorised) or the retailer's. The whole car in the frame, with air round it. Words never on the car — on the road, the ground, the sky or a plate.
  4. Words over a photograph are measured on the composited render (WCAG AA: 4.5, or 3 for headings from 24px). Where a region fails, the words move — never a heavier shade.
  5. The dealer's words or Bentley's, verbatim. No copy of our own, no invented figures or specifications, no placeholder on a published page.
  6. Titles in their own case. Page titles and headings in Title Case, as bentleymotors.com writes them. Capitals belong to model names only (TORCAL, MULLINER). Labels, buttons and routes are uppercase through the CSS, never typed.
  7. Each page keeps its template. Inner pages stay on the inner-page template; the model-page language is for model pages only. A weak page is fixed with better pictures and words inside its template, not a new layout.
  8. The client's four routes are always on screen: Bentley Inventory (prominent), Special Offers, Financing, Contact Us — the header's second line on a desktop, a bar at the foot of a phone's screen.
  9. One form, one card, one price. The same form wherever a form is; the same vehicle card everywhere; the Final price leads (FTC).
  10. Cards in a row are one height, every line level. Wherever cards stand side by side, each card is as tall as the tallest and its lines meet the same lines in the cards beside it (see Vehicle cards).

Vehicle cards

One card everywhere: photograph, name, facts, and the price plate — Final price leads; MSRP and the two dealer charges sit beneath as the working. A lease offer carries its term and the money down (Bentley's word: "down").

Cards in a row — the rule

  • One height. Every card in a row is as tall as the tallest one in it. No card ends short.
  • Lines level across the row: photograph · mark band · kind · name · facts · price · build · extra line · View details. When one card's line runs longer (a kind line on two lines, a two-line name, a third lease line), that line grows in the whole row, so the next line still starts on the same level in every card.
  • No empty band. A card without a mark keeps no room for one: its words start right under its photograph, and what it is shorter by stays above View details. Its lines then sit higher than a marked neighbour's — that is accepted; an empty gap under the photograph is not.
  • View details on the foot line of the card, the same in every card of the row.
  • One photograph shape (4:3, the car whole in the frame, the same crop rule) in every card.
  • Everywhere cards stand side by side: the listing, the home page's New in stock, a model page's In Stock, More cars on the car page, Special Offers.
  • How: CSS subgrid. The list is a grid; each card spans nine of its rows (grid-row: span 9; grid-template-rows: subgrid), the photograph and the band take rows 1–2, the words are a subgrid of the other seven; a card without a mark runs its words from row 2 to the foot in one column, View details at its foot (css/static7.css). Never a fixed card height, never cut text, never an ellipsis.
  • On a phone the cards stand one above another, each at its own height.

Marks under the photograph

  • Never on the photograph — nothing covers the car. One mark at a time, as a band directly under the photograph.
  • Only a marked card has a band. Beside it, an unmarked card keeps no empty room: its words start under its photograph (see Cards in a row).
  • Lease: Heritage White, black type, the payment then its term and money down. Sale Pending: Bentley Green. Sold: Dark Grey (a "Recently sold" record only — a sold car never appears in the listing).
  • Certified Pre-Owned is a band of its own (the page ground, a hairline above) for a certified car with no other mark; with a lease, "Certified by Bentley" stays in the kind line.

Text on photographs

  • Never on the car. The words go where the car is not — the road, the ground, the sky, a lit wall. Choose and position the frame for the words (object-position set per photograph, per breakpoint).
  • White on a dark region, the ink on a light one (a studio wall). On white words: headings 100%, copy 92%, labels 84–88%.
  • Measured on the render, not guessed: the photograph as drawn, with its shade, under each line; the 95th percentile of the pixels must reach 4.5 : 1 for text, 3 : 1 for headings from 24px. A moving loop is measured over every second.
  • A shade only under the words — a corner or two-way gradient of Black (#040404), as the page head and model hero above give it. Never a full-frame scrim, never a box behind the words.
  • Where a region fails, the words move to a darker part of the frame, or the frame changes — never a heavier shade.
